Pricing

Free for the people hiring. Firms pay for distribution — because qualified, pre-matched leads are the thing every specialist firm struggles to get. Target pricing at maturity: some paid capabilities (the relationship graph, code-intelligence reports) only exist once the V2–V3 data phases have generated them.

Developers, corporate end-users, and anyone hiring: free. Always. Marketplace liquidity depends on demand-side volume — charging the buyer side would strangle it.
And one hard rule: nothing a firm pays for ever changes bid rankings or RFP access. Paid tiers buy visibility and tooling — never an edge against the developer's interests.

Why not take a cut of each project?

A success-fee model (1–3% of contract value) is the obvious alternative and stays on the roadmap. The risk: it pushes deals off-platform the moment two parties meet, which kills the data flywheel. Subscriptions keep the incentive aligned — the platform wins when firms stay listed, not when it taxes each transaction. A hybrid (lower subscription + small fee on platform-awarded bids) is the likely end state.

What about land listings?

Land brokers pay per-listing or via Professional plans to put parcels in front of developers matched to that property type and jurisdiction — a second revenue lane that also feeds the demand side with new projects to staff.
